Degree distribution + frame schedule experiment
===============================================

Tool: tools/diag/degree-experiment.ts
Reproduce:  LB_TRIALS=5 npx tsx tools/diag/degree-experiment.ts      (also: npm run diag:degree)

Metric: frames the SENDER must display per source block (sent/K). This is the
quantity that maps directly to transfer time on screen. k = 1024 blocks of 512 B
(512 KiB payload), 5 trials per cell, blockSize = 512 B.

Two schedules are compared for each distribution:
  lt      pure LT stream from frame k+1 (no systematic prefix at all)
  sys+lt  the shipped schedule: a 0.1K systematic prefix (plus its mask frame),
          then the LT stream

Three channels:
  keep = 1.0   perfect optics
  keep = 0.7   30% of frames lost independently (blur, motion, exposure)
  keep = 0.5, bursts of 40 lost frames every 60   (a hand crossing the camera,
          an autofocus hunt) on top of 50% independent loss

The uniform distribution is a control, not a candidate: it draws each frame's
neighbours from the whole file, so a frame XORs ~k/2 blocks and a run is two
orders of magnitude slower. It carries a 400-frame leash (see `maxFrames` in the
script) and its rows therefore read "0% done", which is the finding.

Every number in the sent/K, recv/K and redund/K columns is deterministic: the trials
use fixed seeds (1000 + t*17), so re-running the command above reproduces those columns
exactly. The ms column is wall-clock time on whatever machine ran it and will differ
between machines and runs; it is here only to show that the shipped configuration does
not pay a decode cost for its lower overhead - the ideal-soliton and uniform rows spend
10-100x more time per received frame, because their high-degree frames force large
solves.

Run on 2026-09-24 with node 20.20.2 and tsx 4.23.15. Every number below comes from
this one run, by the current revision of the script - the one that splits the
schedule (lt vs sys+lt), leashes the uniform control with `maxFrames`, and reads
`LB_K` / `LB_TRIALS` / `LB_MAX_FRAMES` from the environment.

k=1024 blocks of 512 B (512 KiB payload), 5 trials per cell

DISTRIBUTION SHAPE (k = 1024)
-----------------------------
robust soliton (c=0.03, delta=0.5): mean degree 10.57, P(1)=0.77%, P(2)=47.6%, P(3)=16.0%, P(4)=8.0%
ideal soliton:                      mean degree  7.51, P(1)=0.10%, P(2)=50.0%, P(3)=16.7%, P(4)=8.3%

The P(1) row is the one that matters. The robust distribution reserves 0.77% of
its mass for degree-1 frames - the frames that finish a source block outright -
against 0.10% for the ideal soliton. That reserve is what stops the peel from
stalling with a handful of uncovered blocks left, and it is the difference between
1.10 and 2.06 frames per block.

CONCLUSION
----------
Robust soliton with c = 0.03 and delta = 0.5 is the shipped distribution, and the
sweep above says why on every channel:

  clean channel      shipped 1.102   c = 0.10  1.174   ideal  1.309   (with prefix)
  clean channel      shipped 1.096   c = 0.10  1.164   ideal  2.059   (pure LT)
  30% loss           shipped 1.554   c = 0.10  1.653   ideal  1.854
  50% + bursts       shipped 3.594   c = 0.10  3.838   ideal  4.203

c = 0.03 beats c = 0.10 in all three channels: a heavier spike at low degrees
starts faster and costs more in the tail, and the tail is what a camera pays for.
The ideal soliton is the instructive number: it is asymptotically optimal and
needs 1.87x the screen time of the shipped distribution when both are measured
without a systematic prefix (2.059 vs 1.096), because its tail has almost no
degree-1 mass. The dense fallback (limit 512) does not rescue it - its rows are
identical to the plain ideal rows in every channel, which is the point of that
comparison.

The systematic prefix is worth its frames under loss: with a prefix the shipped
distribution needs 3.594 frames per block in the hardest channel against 3.830
without one, and 1.554 against 1.588 at 30% loss, while costing 0.006 frames per
block (0.5%) on a clean channel. That trade - a fast cold start and a better tail
for half a percent on a perfect channel - is made in
packages/core/src/fountain/lt.ts, and the prefix length itself is chosen in
docs/prefix-experiment-output.txt.

Ideal soliton remains the fallback for K <= 2, where the robust construction
degenerates and there is no tail to lose.
